# 06 -- What Happens When

Every collaboration has an ending. The good ones plan for it. This section covers common failure modes and the questions you should have already answered before they arrive.

These are conversation prompts and example policies. Adopt or adapt the timeframes and approaches that fit your collaboration. Work through them together and record your decisions in the [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md).

---

## Section 01. Someone stops contributing

This is the most common failure mode. Not a blowup, not a disagreement. Just silence. Life happens.

**Questions to answer now:**

How long does someone go quiet before it triggers a check-in? (Example policy: 30 days of no communication or logged contribution.) Define what "quiet" means for your collaboration: no commits, no communication, no logged hours?

What's the check-in process? A direct message? A standing weekly sync? An async update in a shared doc?

If someone goes inactive beyond the agreed threshold, what happens? Does their share of future revenue change? Does the remaining collaborator gain authority to make decisions solo?

Can the remaining collaborator continue the project alone? (This depends on the intended treatment recorded under [02 Who Owns What](02-who-owns-what.md) and the infrastructure access recorded under [04 Infrastructure and Custody](04-infrastructure-and-custody.md).)

**Build a culture where stepping back is normal, not shameful.** The graceful version is: "I need to step back for a while. Here's what's in flight, here's where the credentials are, here's what I think the project needs next." The [Contribution Log](08-contribution-log.md) makes handoffs possible because the project's state is documented.

---

## Section 02. Someone wants out

Different from going quiet. This is an active decision to leave.

**Questions to answer now:**

What's the notice period? (Example policy: 30 days for a side project.)

Does the departing collaborator transfer their infrastructure access? (See [04 Infrastructure and Custody](04-infrastructure-and-custody.md) and the [access and custody matrix](templates/access-and-custody-matrix.md).)

What happens to their contributions? Refer to the intended treatment recorded under [02 Who Owns What](02-who-owns-what.md) and in the [project asset inventory](templates/project-asset-inventory.md).

Does the departing collaborator retain any share of future revenue? For how long? Under what conditions? Record this in the Working Agreement.

Can the departing collaborator fork the project and build their own version? The answer depends on the project's license and the collaborators' intended treatment of jointly created work.

## Section 04. The project makes no money

Most side projects don't. That's fine. But "no money" changes the emotional math.

**Questions to answer now:**

At what point do you mutually agree to wind down? (A date? A milestone? "When neither of us has committed in 90 days"?)

Who custodies the domain and repo after wind-down? The collaborator who wants to keep it? The one who paid for it? Decide.

Is there a post-mortem? Even a quick "what we learned" doc is valuable for future collaborations.

---

## Section 05. The project makes a lot of money

The happy problem. Money amplifies existing tensions.

**Questions to answer now:**

Does the original split still feel fair at 10x the revenue? 100x?

When do you revisit the split? (Example policy: annually, at revenue milestones, or when roles change significantly.)

At what point does the project need a formal legal entity? This is a formalization trigger (see "When This Kit Stops Being Enough" in the README). Ongoing joint commercial activity and profit sharing may have legal, tax, reporting, or business-structure consequences depending on the jurisdictions involved, even when nobody formally created an entity.

---

## Section 06. One collaborator wants to pivot, the other doesn't

These aren't disagreements about execution. They're disagreements about what the project is.

**Questions to answer now:**

How are strategic decisions made? (Consensus? One collaborator has final say within their domain?)

If you can't agree, what's the tiebreaker? (A trusted third party? A fork?)

Can one collaborator fork the project and take it in their direction while the other maintains the original?

---

## Section 07. A new obligation conflicts with the collaboration

A new employer's IP policy, a client agreement, confidentiality obligations, exclusivity requirements, contractual restrictions, or other commitments may affect a collaborator's ability to participate. The kit does not determine whether those restrictions are legally valid.

**Questions to answer now:**

Is there an obligation to disclose potential conflicts?

What happens to committed work in progress?

Does a material conflict trigger the "someone wants out" process above?

---

## Section 08. How to use this section

Don't negotiate every scenario in advance. Read through these together, discuss the ones most relevant to your situation, and capture the big decisions in the [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md). The goal is not a perfect plan. It's a shared understanding of how you'd handle the hard stuff.
