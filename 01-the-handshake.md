# 01 -- The Handshake

Before you build anything together, each collaborator fills out a contribution inventory. This isn't a contract. It's a conversation framework that makes the invisible visible.

---

## Section 01. Why this matters

Most collaborations fail not because people disagree, but because they never agreed in the first place. "I'll handle the tech side" means something different to everyone. This inventory forces specifics.

---

## Section 02. How to use it

Each collaborator fills out a copy of the [contribution inventory template](templates/contribution-inventory.md) independently, then you compare. The gaps between your two versions are the conversations you need to have.

---

## Section 03. The comparison conversation

After both collaborators fill out the inventory, sit down (or get on a call) and walk through them together. Look for:

**Overlaps.** If both people wrote "I'll handle deployment," you have a territory conflict. Decide now.

**Gaps.** If neither person listed "customer support" or "documentation," those tasks will fall to whoever cares more, and that breeds resentment. Name every unclaimed task and assign it or agree to punt it.

**Asymmetries.** One collaborator brings an existing audience of 10K and three months of full-time availability. The other brings a weekend hobby and a great idea. That's fine, but it changes how you think about [02 Who Owns What](02-who-owns-what.md) and [03 Money](03-money.md). Name the asymmetry out loud.

**Timelines.** "I can do 20 hours a week for 3 months" and "I'll chip in when I can" are not compatible commitments. Get specific or get honest about what this really is.

---

## Section 04. What this produces

Completed inventories that both collaborators have reviewed and agreed represent the starting state. Store the completed inventories in the project's chosen governance-record location. Reference them later when things drift, because they will.
