# 05 -- Expenses

For collaborators without a shared business entity, the line between "project expense" and "personal overhead" is blurry. Get clear before someone spends money and expects reimbursement the other person didn't agree to.

---

## Section 01. What counts as a shared project expense

Decide together. These are common categories to discuss, not the kit's classifications. Where a category lands is your decision to make and record.

**Commonly treated as shared:**

- Domain registration
- Hosting costs beyond whatever no-cost tier the project is using
- API costs directly tied to the shared project
- Tool subscriptions used exclusively for the shared project
- Contractor or freelancer fees for project work
- Payment processor transaction fees

**Commonly treated as personal:**

- Your laptop, your internet, your desk
- General software subscriptions you'd have anyway
- Collaborator time, which is typically treated as a contribution rather than a reimbursable project expense unless the collaborators agree otherwise. Contributions are accounted for in the revenue model in [03 Money](03-money.md).

**Gray areas (decide together):**

- A tool subscription that's partly personal, partly project
- Conference attendance or courses taken "for the project"
- Travel to meet your collaborator

One common way collaborators draw the line: an expense you would have had without the collaboration is often treated as personal, and an expense the collaboration created is often treated as shared. That is a starting point for the conversation, not a classification the kit makes. Decide together what belongs in your model and record it.

## Section 02. Approval thresholds

A common approach is to agree on a spending threshold above which both collaborators approve before the expense is incurred, with either collaborator free to spend and log amounts below it. Whether you use a threshold at all, and where you set it, is your decision to make and record. Whatever you choose should match the decision rights recorded in your Working Agreement.

## Section 03. Pre-revenue expense handling

When the project isn't making money yet, someone is still paying. Options:

1. **Track and offset.** Log every expense as it happens. If you use the optional Financial Foundations module, the [expense tracker](modules/financial-foundations/expense-tracker.md) is built for this. When revenue arrives, reimburse agreed costs before any split, if that is what the collaborators decided in [03 Money](03-money.md).

2. **Stay on low-cost tiers.** Use free or low-cost tiers where the provider's current terms permit your intended use. Don't add recurring cost until the project actually needs the capability. Check the terms, since some no-cost tiers exclude commercial use.

3. **Split costs from day one.** Each collaborator pays half the project expenses monthly. Clean and simple but requires discipline.

4. **One collaborator funds, the other contributes time.** Explicitly asymmetric and that's OK, as long as it's named and accounted for. Document it in [01 The Handshake](01-the-handshake.md) and the [Contribution Log](08-contribution-log.md).

## Section 05. Cross-border expenses

When collaborators are in different countries, expenses may involve different currencies, different tax treatments, and different expectations about what's reasonable. Keep expense decisions jurisdiction-neutral and treat cross-border cost complexity as a professional-advice trigger when it becomes material (see "When This Kit Stops Being Enough" in the README).

---

## Section 06. Recording expenses

Record every shared expense somewhere both collaborators can see. If you use the optional Financial Foundations module, the [expense tracker template](modules/financial-foundations/expense-tracker.md) is built for this. Every shared expense gets a line: date, who paid, what, amount, currency, category, whether it was approved. Review together monthly as part of the [Contribution Log](08-contribution-log.md) review.
