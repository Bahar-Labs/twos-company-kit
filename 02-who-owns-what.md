# 02 -- Who Owns What

This section helps collaborators record their intended treatment of intellectual property. "If we stop working together tomorrow, what can each of us walk away with?" Figure out your intended answers before you write a single line of code together.

Two's Company Kit helps you decide and record how you intend assets to be treated. It does not determine ownership, rights, or legal obligations. Local law may affect whether or how the collaborators' intentions are enforceable. What the kit does is help you think through the questions and record your decisions clearly, so that if you later need professional advice, there's a record of what you intended.

---

## Section 01. The decision framework

For each asset in the shared project, work through these questions together. Each one is a decision for the collaborators to make and record, not a conclusion the kit reaches for you.

### Did it exist before this collaboration?

**Yes.** Decide how the collaborators intend pre-existing work to be treated. A common intended treatment is that pre-existing work stays with the person or entity that brought it, and is made available to the shared project on agreed terms rather than transferred. Record what's being made available, on what terms, and for how long.

**No.** Move to the next question.

### Who created it?

**One collaborator built it during the collaboration.** Decide how you intend this asset to be treated. Options collaborators commonly consider: the creator retains it and the shared project has permission to use it, or both collaborators have agreed rights to it. Record the decision.

**Both collaborators built it together.** Decide what each collaborator intends to be able to do with it. Can either collaborator use it independently, modify it, or build on it outside the shared project? Record the answers rather than assuming a shared default.

**It was built with shared resources.** If project funds paid for it (API costs, contractor fees, tool subscriptions), decide who holds or has custody of the asset and what rights each collaborator intends to retain. Who paid for something and who holds it are separate questions from what rights the collaborators intend each person to have, and the third question is the one your records should answer.

### Is it the core product or a component?

**Core product.** The thing you're shipping together. Work through [03 Money](03-money.md) to decide how revenue is handled. The [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md) should capture your intended treatment.

**Component or module.** If a distinct component or module is created or brought into the collaboration, decide how the collaborators intend it to be treated. Record the creator or source, holder or custodian, intended project-use rights, attribution or branding expectations, and post-exit treatment in the [project asset inventory](templates/project-asset-inventory.md).

A common starting point, and the one this kit is built around, is that the module's creator retains it, the shared project has permission to include and distribute it as part of the project, and the creator's branding and attribution travel with the module. Collaborators frequently adopt this because it lets someone contribute a specialized piece without giving up the ability to use their own expertise elsewhere.

This is a starting point for your conversation, not a determination the kit makes. If you adopt it, record it as your decision in the inventory. If your collaboration needs something different, decide that instead and record that. Either way the answer lives in your records, not in this document.

---

## Section 02. Where the per-asset decisions live

Individual assets and their intended treatment belong in the [project asset inventory](templates/project-asset-inventory.md), which is maintained as a living record. The inventory captures, for each asset, who created or brought it, who holds or has custody of it, where it lives, what the collaborators intend for it, what the project may do with it, and what happens to it on exit.

The [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md) captures the collaboration's overall approach. The inventory captures the asset-by-asset detail. Keep them separate so that adding an asset doesn't require reopening the agreement.

---

## Section 03. When there is no formal entity

Do not assume the shared project itself is a separate legal owner. For each asset the project depends on or whose treatment could matter later, identify the person or entity that holds or has custody of it and record the rights the collaborators intend each person to have.

This is a governance decision the collaborators are making, not a legal conclusion Two's Company Kit is drawing.

## Section 04. Third-party material

Some of what a project depends on belongs to someone outside the collaboration: licensed fonts, stock assets, open-source dependencies, purchased templates, API terms. The collaborators' intended treatment cannot create rights in that material beyond what its license or terms already allow.

For any third-party material, record the applicable license or terms in the [project asset inventory](templates/project-asset-inventory.md), and make sure the project's intended treatment is consistent with the rights actually available under them. If the intended treatment and the license disagree, the license governs and the intended treatment needs to change.

---

## Section 05. If you release the project publicly

If the collaboration releases work under an open-source or open-content license, that license defines what the public can do with the released material. The collaborators' internal governance, meaning who decides to release, who can change the license, and who maintains the project, is a separate question from the public license. Document both.

Agree on the license for your shared project before release and record it in the [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md).

---

## Section 06. The uncomfortable conversation

If one collaborator brings significantly more pre-existing work than the other, that changes the dynamic. Name it. The collaborator bringing an established brand, a working codebase, or a proven methodology is contributing something that took years to build. The collaborator bringing fresh development time is contributing something valuable too, but different. [03 Money](03-money.md) is where you calibrate the approach to reflect this.
