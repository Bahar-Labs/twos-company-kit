# 03 -- Money

Money ruins collaborations that don't talk about money. The conversation is awkward. Have it anyway. Before money arrives, not after.

These are decision models, not legal or accounting recommendations. The right model depends on what each collaborator is contributing (go back to [01 The Handshake](01-the-handshake.md) if this isn't clear).

---

## Section 01. Revenue split models

Pick one, adapt one, or design your own from these building blocks.

### Model 1: Equal split

Both collaborators receive the same share. Simple and clean. Works best when both people are contributing roughly equal time, skills, and pre-existing assets. Falls apart when contributions become lopsided.

### Model 2: Proportional to contribution

Each collaborator's share reflects what they bring. Options for measuring contribution: time logged (hours), value of pre-existing assets made available, revenue generated through a collaborator's distribution channel, or a negotiated percentage based on the Handshake inventory. Requires honest tracking (see [08 Contribution Log](08-contribution-log.md)).

### Model 3: Role-based

Different roles get different shares. "Builder gets 60%, distribution gets 40%" or "core maintainer gets X, component creator gets Y per module." Works well when the roles are clearly different.

### Model 4: Milestone-based

The split changes at thresholds. Early revenue reimburses infrastructure costs. After a certain point, the split shifts. This protects the collaborator funding early costs and motivates both people to hit thresholds.

## Section 02. What actually gets distributed

Before you can split revenue, agree on what you're splitting. Things collaborators commonly consider when defining distributable revenue:

- Refunds and chargebacks
- Payment and transaction processing fees
- Required taxes or withholding where applicable
- Approved shared project expenses (see [05 Expenses](05-expenses.md))
- A project reserve, if the collaborators agree to maintain one

Your project may have different costs or assumptions. The point is to agree together on what deductions apply before money arrives, not to discover a disagreement when the first revenue hits. Record your decisions in the [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md).

---

## Section 03. Payment logistics

No payroll department. No AP. Just two people and their bank accounts.

**Cadence.** Monthly is common. Quarterly works if revenue is low or irregular. Per-transaction is a nightmare. Pick a cadence and put it in the [Working Agreement](07-working-agreement.md).

**Method.** Bank transfer or another agreed payment service. For cross-border payments, agree on the exchange-rate source and timing used for conversions, and record both in the Working Agreement.

**Documentation.** Every distribution gets a line in the [Contribution Log](08-contribution-log.md). Amount, date, what it covers, exchange rate if applicable.

---

## Section 04. Tax, reporting, and related obligations

Tax, reporting, withholding, invoicing, VAT/GST/sales-tax, and related obligations can depend on the collaborators, customers, business structure, and jurisdictions involved. The kit does not determine those obligations. Cross-border activity and meaningful revenue are professional-advice triggers (see "When This Kit Stops Being Enough" in the README).
